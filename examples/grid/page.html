Plate III · Electricity, magnetism and energy

The grid, as a miniature.

Falling water turns a wheel, the wheel spins a magnet inside copper coils, and a flyball governor holds the speed. Transformers raise the voltage for the long line and lower it again for the town, where every window is lit by the same balance of supply and demand.

Key to the plate · follow the energy

From falling water to a lit window

  1. 1Water wheelWater from the tank strikes the buckets. The jet is the turbine's mechanical power.
  2. 2Flyball governorSpinning weights fly out as the shaft speeds up and close the water valve: droop control.
  3. 3RotorAn electromagnet, north pole red and south pole blue, turned by the shaft.
  4. 4Stator coilsFixed copper bars. The sweeping field induces e = NBAω sin ωt in them.
  5. 5Step-up transformerFew turns to many on one iron core. Voltage rises, current falls by the turns ratio.
  6. 6Line conductorsThree phases. They heat by 3I²R; red glow in WebGL shows the loss.
  7. 7Pylon and insulatorsA lattice tower holds the conductors high and apart on strings of glass discs.
  8. 8Step-down transformerThe same machine reversed, back down to the town's 11 kV.
  9. 9The townWindows dim when the voltage sags and go dark when the breaker opens.
  10. 10Frequency meterThe grid's speed. It falls when demand runs ahead of supply; red marks the cut-off.
  11. 11Loss meterPower the line turns into heat. Watch it as you change the line voltage.
  12. 12BreakerDisconnects the town. The machine overspeeds until the governor catches it.

Balance, and why the line runs high

On the left, the last minute of the grid: generation must follow demand, and frequency is the sign of how well it does. On the right, the same demand sent at each line voltage: losses fall with the square of the voltage, which is why lines run at hundreds of kilovolts.

Wall text IV

About this specimen

What is exact

The arithmetic. The swing equation sets frequency from the gap between mechanical and electrical power; a 4 % droop governor and slower automatic control answer it; the line's losses follow 3I²R with I from the power and the voltage, solved exactly, including the limit beyond which a line cannot carry more.

What is set by hand

The sizes: one 150 MW machine with an inertia of 6 s, a line of about 110 km at 9 Ω per phase, and a town of 10 to 140 MW. The machine turns 100 times slower on the bench than in life, and the alternation is drawn at the same slowed rate.

What is left out

Reactive power and power factor, line inductance, the three phases' separate waveforms, and the many other machines a real grid shares its load with. A real grid is far stiffer than one machine on its own.
