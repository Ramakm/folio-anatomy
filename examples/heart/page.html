Plate II · The human heart

The heart, as a double pump.

Two piston pumps share one bench and one beat. The right pump pushes dark blood through the lungs; the left pump pushes bright blood through the body. Four valves keep every drop moving one way.

From the body, round, and back

  1. 1Venous reservoirThe great veins. Blood comes home from the body here, at low pressure and low in oxygen.
  2. 2Right atriumA small primer pump. It fills while the ventricle squeezes, then tops it up with a short kick.
  3. 3Tricuspid valveLets blood fall into the right ventricle. The lever lies along the pipe when open, across it when shut.
  4. 4Right ventricleThe pump for the lungs. It peaks near 25 mmHg; the lungs need only a gentle push.
  5. 5Pulmonary valveOpens while the right ventricle ejects, then seals the pulmonary artery.
  6. 6LungsBellows breathing 14 times a minute. Blood goes in dark and comes out bright with oxygen.
  7. 7Left atriumCollects oxygenated blood from the pulmonary veins.
  8. 8Mitral valveThe left inflow valve. Choose MR and it leaks backwards; a red ring marks the leak.
  9. 9Left ventricleThe pump for the whole body. It peaks near 115 mmHg and moves about 70 mL a beat.
  10. 10Aortic valveOpens once the ventricle out-pushes the aorta. Choose AS and it narrows.
  11. 11Air chamberThe elastic aorta. A cushion of air stores each surge and keeps pressure up between beats.
  12. 12Capillary bedThe body. Seventeen fine tubes where blood gives up its oxygen. Marks move at the true flow.

One beat, in five phases

Every beat runs through the same five phases, and the valves decide which one you are in. A valve opens only when the pressure behind it is higher than the pressure in front. The phase the bench is in now is shaded.

Wall text III

The beat on paper

Physiologists draw the same beat two ways. On the left is pressure against time, the chart Carl Wiggers published in 1915. On the right is pressure against volume: each beat traces a loop, and the area inside is the work the ventricle does. Both are redrawn from the bench after every beat.

Wall text IV

About this specimen

What is exact

The arithmetic. Each chamber is a time-varying elastance, P = E(t)(V − V₀). Arteries and veins are compliances; vessels and valves are resistances, and valves only pass blood forwards. The bench integrates these equations every half millisecond while you watch.

What is set by hand

The parameters, tuned to a resting adult: about 115/80 mmHg, 70 mL per beat and 5 L a minute at 72 beats. The stenosis and leak are stronger than most patients have, so the change is easy to see.

What is left out

Heart muscle, the coronary arteries and the electrical system. The lungs only recolour the blood; breathing is not modelled. The contraction curve is simple, so isovolumic contraction runs about twice as long as in a real heart.
