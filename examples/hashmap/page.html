Plate IV · Data structures

The hash map, as a sorting office.

Each key is read by a press that turns it into a number, and a dial keeps only the low bits of that number to choose a bin. Two keys will sooner or later choose the same bin. Watch the office settle it three ways: hang a chain of cards in front of the bin, or send the carriage on to try other bins, one step at a time or in growing strides.

Wall text IV

About this specimen

What is exact

The arithmetic. Keys are hashed with Java's String.hashCode, spread with h ^ (h >>> 16) and masked with m − 1, exactly as java.util.HashMap does. The probe sequences, the 0.75 load limit, the doubling and the probe counts are computed, not drawn; the curves are Knuth's formulas for keys hashed uniformly.

What is set by hand

The eight fruit and the three absent ones, chosen so that collisions happen early, and the order they arrive in. The table starts at four bins and the bench has room for eight, so after one doubling it fills past 0.75 instead of growing again; that is where the long probe runs show. When it doubles, keys are placed again in the order they first arrived; real tables walk the old array instead.

What is left out

Deletion, which open addressing needs tombstones for; Java's habit of turning a chain of eight or more into a small tree; Python's perturbed probing; Robin Hood, cuckoo and Swiss tables. And the cost of the hash itself, which for long keys can outweigh every probe.
